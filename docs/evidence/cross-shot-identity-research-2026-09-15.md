# Cross-shot identity and calibration: research update

Research checked: 2026-09-15 UTC. Scope: methods relevant to the supplied NFL All-22 footage, not a claim that an external method has been reproduced here. This note supports the implementation plan; it does not promote a tracker or calibrator.

## Conclusion

The appropriate next step is a reviewed, time-varying field-calibration baseline and synchronized cross-view evaluation. Replacing the within-shot tracker alone cannot supply these missing signals. The full-game file can supply representative calibration frames, paired views, difficult identity events, and a held-out within-game test set. It cannot by itself establish performance on different games, uniforms, stadiums, or camera installations.

Existing repository guidance already separates team color, metric field coordinates, and replay identity, and says static per-shot homographies are insufficient during camera pan/zoom. The existing evidence describes cross-shot plumbing and missing real-footage proof; its historical tracker measurements are not independently rerun by this research note. See [accuracy/calibration/replay](../accuracy-calibration-replay.md), [cross-shot evidence](cross-shot-identity.md), and [evaluation plan](../evaluation-plan.md).

## Recommended geometry design (proposal)

1. **Freeze a field model.** Define axes, units, origin, direction, and whether end zones are included. NFL hash marks differ from college markings: use the actual NFL template. The NFL describes a 120 by 53 1/3 yard overall field and hash marks 70 feet 9 inches from the sidelines; derive coordinates once and test them. See [NFL terms](https://operations.nfl.com/rules-officiating/nfl-football-basics/football-terms) and [official field rules](https://operations.nfl.com/the-rules/nfl-rulebook). Repeated yard lines and mirrored field halves require semantic landmark labels, not only visually plausible line fits.
2. **Build reviewed keyframes first.** Label well-spread yard-line/hash/sideline intersections in each camera shot, including early/middle/late pan or zoom states. Separate fitting landmarks from withheld evaluation landmarks. Store original-resolution pixels, frame/PTS, landmark name, field coordinate, review state, and source identity. Check reprojection visually in both directions.
3. **Make validity temporal.** A calibration result should cover a declared frame/time interval with per-frame quality and provenance. No indefinite extrapolation, propagation across cuts, or fallback from a different camera. A fit valid near the snap need not remain valid during a downfield pan.
4. **Propagate only on stationary field evidence.** Mask people and graphics before feature matching. Estimate inter-frame field motion with robust correspondences, periodically refit absolute landmarks, and invalidate when support or validation falls below a declared gate. Consider physical camera-parameter fitting after this baseline. Do not linearly interpolate raw homography entries without a geometric validity test; arbitrary matrix scale makes naive interpolation particularly unsafe.
5. **Distinguish field-fit error from player-position error.** Box bottom-center is a proxy for ground contact. Truncated boxes, jumping players, occluded feet, and piles should lower contact confidence or produce null position. A good landmark fit cannot certify a poor contact point. Pose/mask-supported contact estimation is a later ablation with reviewed contact labels.
6. **Carry uncertainty into matching.** Store calibration support and contact uncertainty with coordinates. Reject or abstain on extrapolated regions and poor conditioning. Geometric candidates should be judged against combined calibration/contact/time error and competing candidates, not a distance threshold alone.

## Recommended identity design (proposal)

- **Scope identity by play first.** One player can have multiple shot-local fragments, while different plays have substitutions and repeated formations. Require an explicit play grouping; keep anonymous within-play identity separate from named roster identity and whole-game identity.
- **Align actual play time.** Use native PTS plus reviewed corresponding events. One snap anchor only estimates an offset under equal playback speed. Review another event to verify rate; represent piecewise mappings or invalidate freezes, edited sections, slow motion, and nonoverlap. Never append replay video time to the original play trajectory.
- **Validate local track purity.** Split contaminated tracklets before cross-view fusion. A mixed tracklet can have a plausible median position and team while joining two real players. Inspect crossings, blocks, piles, exit/reentry, and fragments separately.
- **Aggregate evidence over aligned intervals.** Use multiple valid synchronized positions, motion, and conservative team agreement. Treat weak/missing team evidence as unknown. Preserve competing hypotheses when nearby same-team players remain indistinguishable.
- **Solve constrained assignments with an unmatched option.** Use bipartite assignment for a shot pair, then enforce component-wide consistency before any union. Prevent a chain of individually plausible links from merging two players simultaneously visible in the same shot. Account for multiple nonoverlapping fragments belonging to one player rather than assuming every player has exactly one tracklet per shot.
- **Add jersey/ReID only as measured evidence.** Collect per-crop quality and number distributions, aggregate across sufficiently distinct frames, and preserve unreadable/unknown. Wrong high-confidence OCR should not silently override geometry. Uniform color identifies teams, not teammates; general pedestrian embeddings are not established NFL identity evidence. Named identities require reviewed team/number/roster mapping.
- **Export an explanation for every outcome.** Record candidate evidence, valid aligned sample count, competing score/margin, uncertainty, constraint rejection, accepted links, and abstentions. A score is not a calibrated probability unless its calibration was measured on held-out labels.

## Full-game calibration and identity data plan (proposal)

Start by inventorying shots and candidate same-play view pairs from `data/all-22-lions-rams.mp4`. Do not assume alternating cuts always form a matched pair. Review boundaries, camera type, play grouping, timing, and evaluability. Sample across field positions, both field halves, zoom levels, pans, obstruction, lighting, and crowded/open plays. Include negative examples: wrong play pairs, ambiguous players, missing feet, incomplete views, and calibration failure.

Maintain separate annotations for field landmarks, contact points, within-shot tracks, cross-shot identity, and alignment events. Review identity from synchronized video evidence; do not derive truth from the resolver's own predictions. Keep all views and nearby frames from a play in one split. Fit/tune on development plays, freeze thresholds, and score untouched test plays. Reserve a separate-game gate for generalization; the supplied full game enables only a within-game claim initially.

## Evaluation and implementation priorities

1. **Calibration baseline:** retain the repository's median withheld-landmark error <= 1 yard, p95 <= 2 yards, and >= 90% valid evaluable ground-contact coverage. Report distributions by camera, field region, time, and pan/zoom condition. Measure contact error separately from landmark error; define evaluability before seeing predictions.
2. **Timing baseline:** report residual alignment on events withheld from fitting, rates, valid overlap, and maximum nearest-sample gap. Test known offset/rate and freeze perturbations. Timing error can appear as spatial or identity error.
3. **Tracking baseline:** same real detection cache across trackers; reviewed IDF1/HOTA/association errors, switches, fragmentation, and event-window results. Mask activation is an execution fact, not identity evidence.
4. **Cross-shot gate:** retain the existing conservative no-false-merge requirement, with exact correct/incorrect/missing link counts and coverage on reviewed resolvable players. Report pair-level and final connected-component contamination, plus within-shot consistency after fusion. Zero observed errors on a small test set does not prove zero future risk; publish denominators and uncertainty.
5. **Ablate one layer at a time:** static vs time-varying geometry; one vs multiple alignment anchors; geometry-only vs jersey/ReID; greedy vs constrained assignment. Use oracle reviewed geometry/timing/local tracks to locate remaining bottlenecks without calling oracle results deployment performance.

Suggested order: reviewed data and schema -> calibration validity and motion -> timing -> local track/team quality -> constrained cross-shot matching -> optional jersey/ReID -> sealed evaluation and error review. No external checkpoints, data downloads, uploads, paid services, or training were performed for this note.
